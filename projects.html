ESA ESTEC internship and MSc thesis, 2026

Measuring ocean salinity with a forest satellite

BIOMASS is ESA's first P-band radar mission, designed to weigh the world's forests. I built a pipeline to test whether it can also measure how salty the sea surface is.

It downloads radar products from ESA's MAAP platform, compares the HH and VV polarisations, corrects for wind roughness using ERA5 weather data, and turns the result into a salinity value using the physics of how seawater reflects radio waves. Every scene is checked against the SMOS satellite and the GLORYS12 ocean model.

Along the way I found that one product format stored its image bands in reverse order to its own metadata, which had been quietly swapping HH and VV.

  • P-band SAR
  • ERA5
  • SMOS
  • GLORYS12

ESA ESTEC, 2026

Mapping everywhere BIOMASS has looked

A scanner that works through the full BIOMASS catalogue, over 220,000 products, and boils it down to about 5,000 unique places. Each is classed as ocean, coast or land and plotted on a world map, so finding good ocean scenes became a quick lookup. It survives dropped connections and picks up where it left off.

  • Python
  • STAC
  • ESA MAAP

ESA ESTEC, 2026

Spotting an ocean front in the Pacific

In one equatorial Pacific scene, a patch of the radar image behaved differently from the rest. I cross-checked it against ocean model salinity and satellite temperature data and found it lined up with a real front where fresher, cooler water met the surrounding sea.

  • Wave spectra
  • OSTIA
